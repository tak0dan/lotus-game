# War Chest (AEG, 2018 — D. Thompson & T. Benjamin) — rules summary

Two to four players, ~30 minutes. The summary is for the **two-player** game. Source and caveats: `SOURCES.md`.

## The core idea
Your army is a **bag of coins**. Each coin is *both* a unit and the action you spend: a coin you hold lets you act with that unit type, and using it puts it in your discard. You draw only a few coins a round, so the bag decides what you can do — and you can grow the bag.

## Components (2-player)
74 unit coins, 4 royal coins, 16 unit cards (each unit type has its own special rules), 16 faction control markers, a double-sided **initiative marker**, 4 bags, a hex board with **control locations**.

## Setup
- Each player: a faction, a bag, **1 Royal Coin** (into the bag), and **6 control markers**; one marker goes on each of your **two starting locations**, all other locations start neutral.
- Deal **4 unit cards** to each player (random, or drafted in the advanced game). For each of your four unit types put **2 coins into the bag** and the rest into a **supply** beside the card.
- The initiative marker is flipped; the matching player starts with it.

## Winning
**Place all of your control markers on locations.** (Draw if both players have nothing left but their Royal Coin.) Locations, including starting ones, can be captured.

## A round: two phases
1. **Draw coins** — each player draws **3** coins from their bag (hidden). If the bag runs out, shuffle the discard into a new bag. With fewer than three coins in total you simply get fewer actions.
2. **Use coins** — the initiative holder acts first; players **alternate, one coin per action**, until both hands are empty.

## The nine actions
**Placement (coin goes face-up on the board)**
- **Deploy** — put a coin on an empty location you control; creates that unit. **Only one unit of each type may be on the board at once.**
- **Bolster** — stack another coin of the same type on a unit already on the board. A stack moves as one and loses one coin at a time.

**Facedown discards (coin goes hidden to the discard)**
- **Claim initiative** — take the marker (only once a round; not if you hold it).
- **Recruit** — discard any coin, then take **any one coin from your supply** and put it face-up in your discard (it joins the bag at the next reshuffle). *This is how the bag grows.*
- **Pass.**

**Maneuvers (discard a coin matching a unit on the board, face-up)**
- **Move** — one step to an adjacent empty space.
- **Control** — the unit claims the location it stands on (your marker goes there; an enemy marker is returned to its owner).
- **Attack** — hit an adjacent enemy: **remove one coin** from it permanently (back to the box). A single-coin unit dies; a bolstered unit just shrinks.
- **Tactic** — the special action printed on the unit's card.

The **Royal Coin** has no unit: it can only pay for facedown actions (or the Royal Guard's tactic).

## Example unit abilities (from the digital rules page)
Archer — no normal attack; tactic hits an enemy two spaces away · Crossbowman — tactic hits two away in a straight, empty line · Cavalry — tactic: move then attack · Lancer — tactic: move two in a line then attack (must have a target) · Light Cavalry — normal one-step move or a two-space tactic · Berserker — chains maneuvers, paid by removing its own coins · Ensign — gives another unit a free step · Footman — can field two · Marshall — grants a normal attack to a unit in range · Mercenary — free maneuver when recruiting another Mercenary · Pikeman — attribute triggers on any attack against it · Warrior Priest — a coin drawn by its effect must be used immediately. (Full card text exists only on the physical cards.)

## Variants
Advanced draft (8 cards laid out, snake draft 1-2-2-…), second-hand preview, choose play order, elimination draft.

## Why it matters here
The **control-location win**, **initiative**, **limited actions per round**, and the **coin-as-unit/coin-as-action** idea (paying for an action with the very unit that performs it, and attrition that thins your own bag) are the mechanics the project borrows from. Unlike the Tower Game, there is **no economy, no upkeep, no building** — the supply is the only "recruitment".
