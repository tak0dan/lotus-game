# Jihui — the game, its philosophy, and every scene it appears in
*World of Warcraft: Vol'jin — Shadows of the Horde* (M. A. Stackpole). Page numbers are **PDF pages** of the designer's copy (169-page edition).
Written in my own words with short quotes only. There is **no rulebook**: jihui exists only inside the novel, so the rules below are reconstructed from scenes and are incomplete. See `SOURCES.md`.

The book gives jihui three layers: **(1) the game's mechanics** (one scene), **(2) the philosophy it embodies** — *Tushui* vs *Huojin*, balance — which the narrative keeps returning to, and **(3) the game used as a lens on the real world** (battle, politics, people).

---
## 1. The mechanics (main scene, p.27–29)
- **Board:** flat bamboo grid, **12 × 12**. Each side's pieces start on the **last row**, faced toward the enemy, **turned to their lowest value**.
- **Pieces = six-sided cubes.** Each side has **24** (four groups of six) in a **canister** (red vs black). Faces show **dots = movement** and an **arrow = facing**. Seen: warrior, warlord, archer, kite, knights ("move far"), cavalry, **ship**, **fireship**.
- **One action per turn** — choose one:
  1. move/attack as the **top face** says;
  2. **turn the cube one face**, then move or attack;
  3. **pick up and roll** it for a random face, then play it as-is;
  4. **don't touch the board — draw a new piece** (shake the canister; first cube out enters play; if two fall out the second is removed and the opponent **draws free**).
- **First move:** each shakes out one cube; the higher top face goes first.
- **Fireship:** one *face* of a ship cube. **Destroys anything but is consumed** (removed from play; some players burn the piece). Of a side's six ships **only one can be a fireship**, and **rolling is the only way** to get that face (turning one face can never reach it).
- **Ending:** the stated aim is to eliminate the enemy, but doing so is **ill-mannered, even barbaric**. Normally one side is out-manoeuvred and **yields**; yielding to a superior position is **not dishonourable**; losing to a side with more pieces is no shame. **A standstill — balanced forces — is "the greatest victory."**
- **Design intent (p.28):** *thoughtfulness plus impulsiveness*; **deliberation balanced with chance — and chance can be punished.**
- **Worked example (p.28–29):** Vol'jin refaces an archer; Tyrathan's kite; Vol'jin **draws** and gets the fireship, placing it beside the archer to shore up that flank; Tyrathan adds a warrior (a weak face, but enough) and brings knights up the other flank. Vol'jin sees that one **face-change** turns a warrior into a warlord-killer and that no draw can save both flanks — then Tyrathan stops him drawing (a draw would *inflate* a win that was already decided), and Vol'jin concedes "for his honour." Lesson: **a win you must gamble to avoid is not a win you want.**

## 2. The philosophy (the part most scenes are really about)
**Tushui vs Huojin** — the two pandaren poles:
- **Tushui** = thoughtful, stable, contemplative. **Huojin** = impulsive, decisive, "kill first, sort it out later." (p.24)
- **The water / ship / anchor / crew teaching (pp.19–20):** water = Tushui, the ship's very foundation and reason to exist; the **anchor = Huojin**, the decisive act that saves the ship from the shore; Chen adds the **crew** — those who *choose when to sail* and so do the discovery. (Taran Zhu's follow-up: the teapot is a ship with an anchor handle; he will accept no more ships in his harbour — two visitors "are of a balance.")
- **Balance is the aim, not either pole** (p.24–25, p.30, p.35): the Horde (Huojin) and Alliance (Tushui) are in balance; Vol'jin's and Tyrathan's arrival tipped it; "restore the balance and you encourage healing." Troll and man are kept together *because* they're opposite enough to balance. In the **stained-tunic scene (p.30)** Vol'jin's stubborn scrubbing (Huojin) is answered by Tyrathan quietly taking the tunic away (Tushui) — balance restored "**without ego or a need for victory**" — the same ethos as jihui's honourable yield.
- **Both extremes are failures (p.87):** pure Huojin makes all life valueless ("a faceless enemy at a distance is easier to kill than one a sword's length away" — Tyrathan fears he is becoming that); pure Tushui is paralysis, so "not the antithesis of evil." Hence "the monks stressed balance." **Tyrathan calls jihui "a hunter's game — at least the way I play."**
- **Balance is not mere opposition (p.132):** Brother Cuo: peace is not the opposite of war in practice — *violence is balanced by violence of an equal nature moving the opposite way*; "what is the damage done by a sword stroke that cuts nothing?"
- Other statements: monks fight "to embody balance," a shadow hunter "masters chaos" (p.31); archery is studied *for balance* — distance, momentum, arc, breeze (p.62); "**war be imbalance**; if it destroys your balance, your balance was false" (p.62); false balance — people who talked themselves into believing they'd arrived (p.94); fear and hatred offset by respect, peace of camouflage vs war's terror (p.90); loyalty vs blind duty (p.104); Vol'jin to the Shado-pan: "you be the water that lets the ship sail, and the anchor that stops it going too far" (p.124).

### The fireship in the world (pp.127–129)
Of a harbour of Zandalari ships being turned into troop carriers, Vol'jin picks **one** — "I be thinking you'll be happy you taught me jihui." He boards it in disguise (a ratcatcher's patter), kills the magazine guard, **spills powder along the bulkhead to the aft cabin, lights two lamps**, and slips out of a window. **Tyrathan's arrow, shot through the open window, ignites it** — "Jihui. The fireship." The ship is **utterly destroyed**, and its falling masts, guns and wreckage wreck the dock and **a second ship**. It maps exactly onto the game piece: **one of six, only reachable by a deliberate gamble, destroys everything around it, consumed in the act.** Even Vol'jin earlier (p.29) imagines himself as "the fireship, not yet turned to display his maximum power."

## 3b. Beyond the one book — what the wikis and other sources add
The Warcraft Wiki's *Jihui* page (summary of all official appearances) **agrees with the Vol'jin scene on the rules** and adds the following. Nothing there contradicts the notes above, and no source gives more mechanics than the novel does.
- **Stated purpose: "for both players to win"** — more precisely, to leave **both sides in balance**. Anduin Wrynn says so while playing in **Mists of Pandaria**'s quest *Secrets of the First Empire* (patch 5.2, March 2013): asked why he made a move, "the object is for both players to win," and **Wrathion finds that ridiculous** (a cunning player's view vs the pandaren view). The wiki page for *Meet Me Upstairs* (the quest before it) only says they sit at a table with a board game; neither quest page names the game — the jihui link comes from the wiki's own page.
- **Setup details the wiki states explicitly:** two players, **black and red**; 24 six-sided pieces each in cylindrical canisters; piece kinds **archers, cavalry, kites, knights, warriors, warlords** plus a **navy of six ships**; each side shakes out **six cubes** onto the last row at their lowest value, plus one extra cube each to decide who moves first.
- **Who plays:** "one of the most common games in Pandaria" — played in inns across the Jade Forest, Kun-Lai Summit and the Vale (the wiki lists the venues: Honeydew Village inn, Temple of the Jade Serpent, Binan Brew & Stew, The Lucky Traveller, Summer's Rest, Tavern in the Mists…). Outsiders who learn it: **Vol'jin, Tyrathan Khort, Wrathion, Anduin**.
- ***War Crimes*** (Christie Golden, ch. 27): **Wrathion keeps a jihui board for himself** after Pandaria. (A Polish edition scan on the Internet Archive contains a passage about drawing jihui pieces from a bag; I could not read the English text — see sources.)
- **Light's Gambit** (a *The War Within* quest-minigame, Arathi/Hallowfall): **Anduin says it reminds him of jihui** and suggests teaching jihui to Faerin Lothar, who "would likely find its approach to victory as interesting or bewildering as Wrathion did." Light's Gambit is a *different* game — "the Arathi game that teaches military strategy": pieces named Castle (strong ranged attack, cannot move), Knight (Divine Explosion, Holy Assault), Cleric (Heal, Smite); you win by being the only side with pieces on the board — i.e. the **opposite ethos** to jihui's standstill.
- **Pandaren Game Board** (item, patch 5.0.4): an archaeology artifact crafted from 40 fragments; flavour text says pandaren love games and this has been a favourite since the first pandaren emperor. **No rules** on the page; jihui only in "See also."
- The wiki's *Board game* page lists the in-universe games: **Jihui, Pandaren Game Board, Hawks and Hares, Chess Event, Hearthstation** (names only).

**Why it matters:** the game's *explicit aim* — mutual benefit, a balanced standstill — is shown as an **in-universe cultural contrast** (Wrathion's incredulity, Light's Gambit's win-by-elimination). A design that rewards *only* winning would be playing Light's Gambit, not jihui.

## 4. How it lines up with the Lotus manifesto and the Tower Game (my reading — please confirm)
| Jihui | Lotus manifesto / project |
|---|---|
| six-faced cubes; face = strength | every unit's **ID is a D6 value**; four levels, "changing the die or level marker" |
| turn one face, then act | **a kill gains one level**; "keep at max or reroll" |
| roll for a random face | **the Ritual (roll 5): sacrifice, then rerolls** |
| the fireship — one in six, reached only by rolling, destroys anything, consumed | **the Lotuses** — cannot be recruited, only reached through the ritual; powerful but conditional; **sacrificable** |
| draw a piece instead of moving | **summoning by the die** |
| honourable yield; a **standstill** is the greatest victory | manifesto has **no victory condition** ("an open design decision") — *this may be why* |
| Tushui ⇄ Huojin, balance | manifesto §2–3: "long-term planning over impulsive expansion", "every decision creates another problem for the opponent", **"power attracts dependence"** |
| "chance can be punished"; a choice to gamble | bounded, *chosen* randomness — not a roll at the start of every turn |

**Tower Game angle:** simultaneous secret planning is the Tushui half; the reaction/clash resolution and rushing are the Huojin half. Playtest 1's **turtle-draw (X4)** is, in jihui's terms, a *standstill* — honourable, even "the greatest victory." The **hidden-path unit** idea (the fireship is easy to overlook because only one action reaches it) matches your "deliberate gaps" principle.
