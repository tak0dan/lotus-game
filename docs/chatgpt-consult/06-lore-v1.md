AURELIA
Document purpose: Establish the historical foundation, playable institutional powers, visual identities and cultural interpretations of the world. This document is intended to serve three disciplines simultaneously:
    Miniatures design: silhouettes, equipment, materials, heraldry and unit differentiation.
    Card illustration: colours, iconography, faction identity and narrative motifs.
    Rules writing: institutional context, terminology and consistency with existing mechanics.
Central design principle: Nobody is fighting for the fate of the world. They are fighting because the institutions that survived the end of the world still require resources, territory, manpower and obedience.
There are no chosen ones. There are no divine interventions. There are no prophecies that make events happen.
There are only consequences.
I. THE HISTORY OF THE FALL
Calendar
Aurelia uses the Silence Reckoning (SR).
    BS — Before Silence: Years preceding the collapse of the Architects' civilisation.
    AS — After Silence: Years following the collapse.
Year 0 AS marks the failure of the central Architect administrative and communication system, conventionally called the Silence.
Dates are reconstructed from surviving military inventories, inscriptions, cathedral records and taxation ledgers. Some institutions disagree about individual dates, but the sequence of events is generally accepted.
Eight historical entries

1. 840 BS — The Age of Measure
The Architects of Silence emerge as an administrative and engineering civilisation. They unite Aurelia through standardised construction, resource accounting, military logistics and controlled magical infrastructure.
Their greatest achievement is not a weapon but a system: roads, towers, archives and institutions capable of operating across enormous distances.
Cause: Fragmented territories cannot maintain large-scale infrastructure independently.
Consequence: Authority becomes centralised. Local rulers increasingly become administrators of Architect policy.

2. 610 BS — The Great Silence
The Architects introduce the Silence: a continent-wide network of magical communication and administrative coordination. It connects towers, archives, resource stations and military authorities.
The system is designed to eliminate conflicting orders, redundant expenditure and unauthorised local decisions.
Its name comes from its defining characteristic: independent voices and signals become unnecessary.
Cause: The growing complexity of the civilisation exceeds the capabilities of conventional administration.
Consequence: Entire regions become dependent on a network whose internal workings only a small number of Architect institutions understand.

3. 0 AS — The Silence Fails
A chain of failures in the central network causes synchronisation to collapse. Regional towers receive contradictory instructions. Resource allocations become inconsistent. Military garrisons continue following outdated commands.
The system does not explode. It simply stops agreeing with itself.
Within months, central administration ceases to function. The Architects lose the ability to coordinate their civilisation.
Cause: Excessive centralisation, interdependent infrastructure and inadequate independent recovery systems.
Consequence: Aurelia's institutions survive, but their common authority does not. Local commanders, custodians and administrators assume control of whatever remains operational.

4. 19 AS — The Dismantling
The great cathedrals, once centres of worship, administration and magical infrastructure, are systematically dismantled.
Their iron frameworks, bells, chains and structural supports are removed. Stone is reused for fortifications. Decorative metal is melted down.
Some institutions attempt to preserve their cathedrals. Others consider them an unacceptable waste of material.
Cause: Surviving settlements lack the iron necessary for tools, weapons, fortifications and machinery.
Consequence: Cathedral iron becomes the foundation of military industry. The Siege Engine eventually emerges from this practice: a weapon built from the remains of the civilisation it destroys.

5. 47 AS — The First Iron Wars
The first organised wars of the post-Architect age begin.
The initial conflicts are not primarily ideological. They concern mines, foundries, surviving resource stations and the transportation routes connecting them.
Military forces begin to develop specialised administrative structures for recruitment, provisioning, maintenance and casualty replacement.
Cause: Resource scarcity combined with the collapse of central distribution.
Consequence: War becomes an institution. Military strength is measured not only by the number of soldiers but by the ability to feed, equip and replace them.

6. 83 AS — The Exile of the Dragons
Before the Fall, dragons were recorded in high-altitude habitats and were associated with the upper reaches of Aurelia's atmosphere.
Architect experiments with the Silence's atmospheric resonance infrastructure disrupted the pressure patterns and thermal currents used by these creatures to remain aloft.
Their migration routes collapsed. Many descended into the lower atmosphere, exhausted and increasingly dependent on elevated structures as resting places.
Cause: The environmental consequences of the Architects' large-scale atmospheric engineering.
Consequence: Dragons become associated with towers. Their inability to sustain long flights transforms them from inhabitants of the sky into creatures dependent on the architecture of the ground.

7. 126 AS — The Age of Institutions
Surviving organisations formalise their authority.
Monastic schools become military orders. Religious custodians become War Priests. Surveyors become territorial authorities. Resource administrators become independent powers.
Most no longer remember their original purpose accurately.
Cause: Generations of isolation and the need to organise permanent defence and resource extraction.
Consequence: Aurelia develops distinct institutional powers rather than unified kingdoms. Allegiance is increasingly determined by profession, doctrine and institutional obligation.

8. 214 AS — The Age of Claims
The remaining territories have become sufficiently organised to sustain prolonged military campaigns.
Towers are constructed, expanded and occupied. Resource nodes become permanently guarded. Military orders establish standardised recruitment and deployment procedures.
The old Architect infrastructure is no longer something to restore. It is something to possess.
Cause: Institutional consolidation and the development of independent military economies.
Consequence: The present age. Aurelia is divided between powers that all consider their own administration the only rational foundation for civilisation.
Historical truths
These are important boundaries for future lore:
    The Architects did not disappear because of a supernatural curse. Their civilisation failed through systemic dependency.
    The cathedrals were not simply religious buildings. They were infrastructure, administrative centres and material reserves.
    The Dragons were not banished by a god. Their exile has a physical explanation, although the surviving cultures interpret it differently.
    No current institution possesses the complete knowledge of the Architects.
    The present conflicts are not a continuation of one ancient war. They are the result of institutions competing over the same finite resources.
II. THE EIGHT PLAYABLE POWERS
Design philosophy
The eight powers are not eight nations.
They are eight surviving institutional traditions that developed independent military capabilities. They possess territories, fortifications and supply networks, but their authority does not depend on hereditary kingship.
A player commands an institution, not an ethnicity.
This allows two armies of the same institutional origin to fight each other without creating a lore contradiction: institutions can fracture, schism, disagree over doctrine or simply compete for resources.

Institution: The Iron Fists, also called the Fists of the Renegade or the Order of Heavy Metal.
Origin: Originally a collection of independent monastic schools established to preserve physical disciplines after the Silence. Their teachings gradually became militarised as their monasteries were forced to defend their Spirit-gathering grounds.
How they wage war: They favour compact formations, sudden engagements and highly trained individual combatants. Their Monks are not conventional infantry. They are specialists trained to enter an engagement, deliver overwhelming physical punishment and withdraw before the opponent can respond.
Belief: The body is the only instrument that cannot be delegated. Discipline is the only reliable form of faith.
Motto: "The body is truth. The path is law. Discipline is faith."
Banner: Matte black with a single angular yellow fist. Yellow should be bright and unmistakable. No gold ornamentation.
Miniature identity: Black cloaks, yellow markings, chainmail underneath worn leather school jackets, heavy plated gauntlets. Their armour is functional, repaired and visibly used. They do not wear decorative knightly plate.

Institution: The Verdant Warrant.

The overall silhouette should communicate disciplined military archery rather than traditional woodland fantasy elves.

V. QUESTIONS FOR LORE v2
Five decisions will have a particularly large impact on the next version.
1. Institutional independence
Are the eight powers permanently separate institutions, or can individual powers fracture into competing branches?
For example, could two Iron Fist armies fight each other because they follow different interpretations of the same doctrine?
2. The Architects
Were the Architects an actual ruling species or civilisation of ordinary people with extraordinary technological and magical capabilities?
This determines whether their remains, architecture and records should resemble an extinct civilisation or the abandoned infrastructure of a previous human age.
3. Dragons
Is the Dragon a naturally occurring species that existed before the Architects, or was it somehow created, modified or domesticated by them?
The current version assumes a natural species whose habitat was damaged by Architect engineering.
4. Magic
How visible and physically destructive should magic be?
Should Aether and Ember magic look like controlled physical phenomena, with geometric Architect mechanisms and measurable effects, or should it retain a more mystical appearance despite its deterministic nature?
5. Faction gameplay identity
Should the eight powers eventually receive mechanically distinct bonuses and disadvantages, or should their differences remain entirely visual and narrative?
This is particularly important for the relationship between institutional identity and competitive balance.
