THE TOWER GAME — MAPS v2
Aurelia · Continental map specification · 10 November 2026
0. Revised design canon
MAPS v2 retains the three-layer generation architecture from v1:
    Continental skeleton: continental silhouette, impassable barriers, hub and sector boundaries.
    Regional geography: biome placement, landmarks, terrain and regional identity.
    Strategic placement: fortresses, crossings, resource nodes, strategic points and tower-building sites.
The key change is that geography is now subject to explicit mechanical and data constraints.

0.2 Resolving regional identity versus symmetry
I propose separating mechanical geometry from regional appearance.
Each sector has a canonical mechanical template containing:
    Exactly 40 resource nodes.
    Exactly 36 strategic points.
    Four border crossings.
    Equal numbers of each resource type.
    Equal numbers of highland tower sites.
    Equal numbers of viable tower-building squares.
    Equal movement connectivity and access to the central hub.
The sectors then receive different regional skins.

Identity
The Drowned Testament is the remnant of an imperial province swallowed by a catastrophic flood. Its western territory consists of salt-stained agricultural ruins, abandoned cathedrals, broken defensive works and flooded urban districts. The old imperial roads remain partially intact, connecting the provincial fortress to the central capital. Deep water divides the province from its neighbours, but ancient stone causeways and mountain passages preserve the routes between them.
The map is deliberately designed around a mixture of open territory, inaccessible water, elevated ruins and marshland.
1.1 Coordinate and geometry conventions
    Board: 237 × 237.
    Coordinates are normalised fractions of board side.
    Origin: northwest corner.
    X increases eastward; Y increases southward.
    Board centre: (0.5, 0.5).
    Fortress: (0.14, 0.5).
    Sector A: western 90° wedge.
    Remaining sectors: rotations of 90°, 180° and 270° around the centre.
The western sector boundary is:

This describes the western diamond-shaped sector, rather than incorrectly treating it as a rectangular quadrant.
For a point (x,y), a clockwise 90° rotation is:

R(x,y)=(1−y,x)
All other sectors are generated by applying this transformation repeatedly.

Implementation conventions:
    Terrain polygons use ordered vertices.
    Ellipses use their two opposite bounding-box corners.
    Lines use their centreline and width.
    Widths are fractions of board side.
    Crossing coordinates represent the centre of the crossing.
    Node indices are zero-based and refer to their position in nodes.
    nextToNode identifies the resource node adjacent to the strategic point.

4. Five questions for MAPS v3
1. Is 36 strategic points per player definitely intentional?
That means 144 strategic points on the four-player board. With a victory condition requiring more than half, a player would need 73 points if playing individually. Is that the intended strategic density, or should 36 be the total for the whole board?
2. Are resource nodes allowed to be adjacent to each other?
My preference is to allow clusters, but prohibit excessive concentration. For example, no more than three resource nodes within a 5×5 area, except designated resource-rich landmarks.
3. Should strategic points have different geographical classes?
For example:
    Pass control points.
    Religious landmarks.
    Resource-region control points.
    Central imperial ruins.
    Highland tower positions.
All could have identical victory value while differing in tactical importance.
4. How should the central hub's resources be distributed?
Should the hub contain additional neutral nodes, or should all 40 nodes per sector include everything within that sector's inner approach? This affects the balance of early expansion and central conflict.
5. Should sector skins be randomly assigned or deliberately authored for each map?
Random assignment creates replayability, while authored assignments create stronger geographical identities and lore consistency. A hybrid system could select from several compatible regional skins.
5. Important implementation issues before MAPS v3
There are three things I would not consider finished yet:
    Raster validation: The supplied JSON needs automated polygon clipping, node validation and exact grid conversion. The coordinate lists are a starting specification, not a verified playable board.
    Impassable coverage: The 17% target must be calculated from the final rasterised rock and water cells, excluding overlapping shapes.
    Mechanical equivalence: Equal node and point counts alone do not guarantee fairness. We also need to measure distances to crossings, highlands, tower sites and the hub.
The next iteration should ideally produce a validated map schema with explicit grid-cell coordinates, rather than relying exclusively on fractional coordinates.
The objective for MAPS v3 should be to turn this design into a reproducible map-generation specification that can be implemented and tested without interpreting prose.
