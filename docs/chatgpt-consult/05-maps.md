# ChatGPT consult 5 — five 75x75 map concepts (fresh temporary chat, 2026-11-10)

Prompt: the mirrored 75x75 rules (nodes, points next to nodes, towers, dragons) and a request for five original maps, regions, resource clusters, 14-18 points per half, terrain ideas, tower-layout advice and an ASCII sketch. Verbatim answer (shopping/ad link lines stripped):

Aurelia: Five strategic map designs

The maps should not simply be different arrangements of resource squares. They should create different strategic problems.

Your rules already produce three competing forms of territorial control:

    Economic control: resource nodes generate cards, but harvesting units become stationary.

    Military control: towers protect territory, establish defensive lines, and create dragon landing positions.

    Victory control: strategic points require both a tower and a garrison, meaning that occupying them consumes resources and military capacity.

The interesting part is that these three systems conflict. A resource node is valuable because it produces something, but a strategic point next to it requires a separate commitment. A tower is useful for defence, but building too many towers in the wrong places creates an immobile defensive network.

I would therefore design maps around geographical problems: ruined infrastructure, natural barriers, old military roads, sacred places, and industrial remains.

1. The Broken Crown

Recommended starting map

Identity

Once the religious and administrative heart of Aurelia, the Broken Crown is now a vast field of dismantled cathedrals, abandoned monasteries, collapsed aqueducts, and ruined settlements. The central cathedral was deliberately dismantled after the Fall, and its stones were transported across the surrounding countryside to build fortifications. What remains is a landscape of elevated foundations, broken walls, quarries, and burial grounds. The map is relatively open, but the ruins create natural defensive positions. Whoever controls the old religious infrastructure gains access to Spirit resources, while the abandoned stoneworks provide opportunities for establishing tall tower networks.

2. The Drowned Testament
Water and restricted movement
Celephaïs Dead Cities Steamissä

Identity

The Drowned Testament was once Aurelia's largest coastal city. When the sea reclaimed the lowlands, its streets became canals, its plazas became shallow lakes, and its towers became isolated islands of stone. The old city is still recognisable from above: a network of flooded avenues, raised districts, bridges and drowned courtyards. The surviving dry ground is extremely valuable because it determines where armies can move and where towers can be constructed. This map is less about occupying open territory and more about controlling access between separated districts.

What makes this map interesting: Water should block ordinary movement and tower construction. Bridges and raised districts become the only practical routes for heavy units and siege engines. 3. The Ashen Meridian
Open warfare and high ground

Identity

The Ashen Meridian is the scar left by a magical catastrophe that burned an entire province. The land is mostly open, covered in black ash and volcanic glass, with occasional obsidian ridges, dead forests and abandoned fortresses. The region has very few natural obstacles, but its elevated volcanic formations provide exceptional defensive and dragon-perching positions. Resource deposits are scattered across the old industrial settlements and the remains of magical extraction facilities. The map rewards mobility and forward expansion, but a careless player can expose an entire army to ranged attacks.

Identity

The Blackwood Reliquary is a dead forest surrounding the remains of an ancient civilisation. Its trees have not grown leaves for centuries, and their roots have consumed roads, walls and entire settlements. Beneath the forest lies a network of buried temples and forgotten burial chambers. The forest is not uniformly impassable: narrow clearings, old roads and ruined stone courtyards form natural corridors. Building a tower requires finding a suitable clearing, making expansion more deliberate. Meanwhile, Spirit resources are unusually abundant, and the region is an excellent environment for armies built around Monks, Priests and defensive towers.

Vertical warfare and tower networks

Identity

The Last Meridian was once Aurelia's northern defensive frontier: an enormous system of mountain fortresses, elevated roads, bridges, watchtowers and fortified passes. When the empire collapsed, the roads fragmented and entire sections of the mountain infrastructure fell into the ravines. What remains is a landscape of plateaus, cliffs, narrow passes and abandoned military construction sites. The map is deliberately asymmetric in its terrain distribution, although its strategic layout remains perfectly mirrored. The most important locations are not necessarily the richest; they are the places where a tower network can dominate an entire valley. This is the map where the tower-level and dragon systems should have the greatest impact.
